      Highly conductive argyrodite electrolytes are considered to be one of the most prospective solid electrolytes for all-solid-state batteries. However, poor electrochemical compatibility with a Li anode restrains their application. Herein, oxygen doping is adopted to improve the chemical and electrochemical performance of the argyrodite electrolyte. Meanwhile, the Cl−/S2− ratio is increased to enhance the lithium ionic conductivity. The resultant Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte exhibits a high conductivity of 7.49 mS cm−1. Benefitting from the stable Li3OCl formed at the electrolyte/Li interface and the low electronic conductivity arising from the oxygen doping, a Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte shows excellent interfacial stability and lithium dendrites suppression capability. A Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li cell can maintain stable Li plating/stripping for 13 000 h at 0.1 mA cm−2. Moreover, a high critical current density up to 1.3 mA cm−2 of Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 is realized. Consequently, the LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li batteries achieve remarkable better cycling stability than that using pristine Li6PS5Cl, possessing a reversible capacity of 104.6 mAh g−1 at 1C with a capacity retention of 86.7% after 300 cycles.

      Topics
      Doping, Superconductivity, Materials synthesis and processing, Transmission electron microscopy, Solid-state batteries, Chemical elements, Electrolytes, Interfaces, Ionic conductivity, Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy

      All-solid-state lithium batteries coupled with metallic Li anodes and solid electrolytes are attractive for large-scale energy storage, thanks to their high safety and superior energy density.1 To meet the requirements of all-solid-state lithium battery applications, solid electrolytes should possess high room temperature ionic conductivities and good electrochemical compatibility with a Li anode in order to realize high performance batteries.2–6 

      So far, several inorganic solid electrolyte systems, including sulfides, nitrides, hydrides, halides, and oxides, show great potentiality, and the sulfides and oxides are the most studied.7–13 In comparison with O2−, the larger ionic radius and higher polarizability of S2− result in larger Li+ migration channel size and less attraction to Li+ in sulfide electrolytes, which contributes to the higher ionic conductivities of sulfide electrolytes. Several sulfide solid electrolytes, such as Li9.54Si1.74P1.44S11.7Cl0.3 (25 mS cm−1),14 Li5.3PS4.3ClBr0.7 (24 mS cm−1),15 and Li6.6Si0.6Sb0.4S5I (24 mS cm−1),16 have shown higher conductivities than commercial liquid electrolytes.

      Lithium argyrodite electrolytes with low cost and high lithium ionic conductivity are an important family of sulfide electrolytes.17,18 The Li6PS5X (X = Cl, Br, and I) argyrodite sulfide electrolytes derived from Ag+ ions' conductor Ag8GeS6 has received extensive attention due to their high ionic conductivity and low cost.19 The initial reported conductivities of Li6PS5Cl, Li6PS5Br, and Li6PS5I are 1.9 mS cm−1,20 0.7 mS cm−1,21 and 1.3 × 10−6 S cm−1,22 respectively. Further conductivity enhancement of Li6PS5X could be achieved via adjusting the structural disorder between the S2−/X− positions.23 For Li6PS5Cl, the increasing of the Cl−/S2− ratio is able to significantly enhance the lithium ionic diffusivity in the lattice. Li5.4PS4.4Cl1.6 (10.8 mS cm−1),24 Li5.5PS4.5Cl1.5 (12 mS cm−1),25 and Li5.7PS4.7Cl1.3 (6.4 mS cm−1)26 with significantly enhanced conductivities have been reported with increased Cl−/S2− ratios. However, even those electrolytes exhibit high lithium ionic conductivities greater than 5 mS cm−1, and their application still faces many challenges, largely due to interfacial problems.27–29 

      The merit of all-solid-state batteries is the potential to use lithium as the anode, which would significantly improve the energy density. Unfortunately, the PS43− tetrahedral units tend to decompose to LixP and Li2S once directly contacting Li6PS5Cl with lithium, which is associated with the growth of lithium dendrites caused battery failure.30–32 In our previous works, P2O5, ZnO, or Sb2O5 doping strategy in Li3PS4 have been demonstrated to improve the interfacial stability of sulfide electrolytes against Li metal.33–35 In addition, some studies showed that O substitution of S in Li6PS5Br tended to improve its overall stability and electrochemical compatibility to the lithium anode.36 By incorporating ZnO in Li6PS5Br, favorable interfacial stability, dendrite suppression capability, and air stability could be obtained without degrading the ionic conductivity.37 For Li6PS5Cl electrolytes, which show higher conductivity than Li6PS5Br, the theoretical study proposed that substitution of S with O is identified as a fundamentally dependable way to enhance stability against lithium.38 

      In this work, oxygen doping was adopted in Li6PS5Cl to improve the interfacial stability between the Li6PS5Cl electrolyte and lithium anode. Meanwhile, the Cl−/S2− ratio was adjusted for the conductivity enhancement of the oxygen-doped electrolyte. The Li6+xPS5–2xO2xCl1+x (x = 0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.075, and 0.1) electrolytes were prepared, and the highest ionic conductivity of 7.49 mS cm−1 could be obtained from Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05. In addition, the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte shows excellent stability with lithium as well as lithium dendrites suppression capability due to the formation of Li3OCl at the interface. Moreover, the LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li battery delivered a discharge capacity of 117.9 mAh g−1 at 0.1C after 100 cycles, which was markedly better than the battery using the pristine Li6PS5Cl, and exhibited a discharge capacity of 104.6 mAh g−1 at 1C with a capacity retention of 86.7% after 300 cycles.

      The Li6+xPS5–2xO2xCl1+x (x = 0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.075, and 0.1) electrolytes were prepared through a ball milling method followed by annealing. The systematically measured conductivities and Nyquist plots under different experimental parameters are shown in Figs. 1(a) and S1. The highest conductivity of 7.49 mS cm−1 could be acquired with a doping amount of 0.05 mol at 560 °C for 4 h. For Li6+xPS5–2xO2xCl1+x electrolytes with x = 0, 0.025, 0.075, and 0.1, the optimum sintering temperature is 560 °C, and the ionic conductivities are 6.08, 6.45, 6.55, and 5.85 mS cm−1, respectively. Figure 1(b) exhibits the Arrhenius plot of the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte, and the calculated activation energy value is 0.28 eV. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) results of Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 under different temperatures are shown in Fig. S2. In general, the electron conduction within solid electrolytes could also lead to the dendrite growth.39 In order to assess the possibility of dendrite growth, the electronic conductivities of Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 were measured (Fig. S3), which are 1.38 × 10−8 and 1.06 × 10−8 S cm−1, respectively. The reduced electronic conductivity suggests the oxygen doping is beneficial for the dendrite growth suppression.

      X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Raman measurements were conducted to analyze the influence of oxygen doping and Cl−/S2− ratio on phase composition. Peaks in XRD patterns of Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 primarily correspond to a cubic argyrodite phase (F43m), while the peak of Li2S at around 27° was also detected, which could ascribe to the shortened milling time40 [Fig. 2(a)]. It is worth noting that Li3PO4 and other oxides could not be found in Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05, indicating that oxygen ions have entered into the lattice. In addition, the Raman results of Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes are shown in Fig. 2(b), and the PS43− group at 427 cm−1 further confirms the argyrodite structure.41 After oxygen doping, the red shift of the peaks could be observed, which suggests that the vibration energy of PS43− group was decreased. This phenomenon indicates the soften of the lattice and the reduction of the binding on the transport of Li+, which promotes the Li+ migration and results in the higher ionic conductivity of Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes.22,42

      The Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 particles display a uniform size of 2–5 μm [Figs. 3(a) and 3(b)], which is beneficial for the reduction in the interfacial resistance with lithium anodes.43 The crystal structure of Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 was further confirmed by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). The interplanar distances of 0.325 and 0.428 nm correspond to the lattice fringes of the (2 2 0) and (2 0 0) planes of the argyrodite structure, respectively [Fig. 3(c)]. The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern [Fig. 3(d)] also presents diffraction rings corresponding to the argyrodite phase. Moreover, the scanning transmission electron microscopy energy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (STEM-EDS) elemental mapping images of the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte particle reveal that phosphorus, sulfur, chlorine, and oxygen elements are homogenously distributed in the electrolyte particle.

      The stability of electrolytes against lithium was characterized by monitoring the overpotential fluctuation of the Li/electrolytes/Li symmetric cells. The symmetric cells were cycled under step-increased current densities under 25 °C. Figure 4 shows the critical current density tests of Li/Li6PS5Cl/Li and Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li cells. The overpotential of the Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li cell is lower than that of the Li/Li6PS5Cl/Li cell, and the maximum current density that the Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li cell could withstand is determined to be 1.3 mA cm−2, while the critical current density of the Li/Li6PS5Cl/Li cell is 0.6 mA cm−2. Moreover, cyclic performance of the symmetric cells with Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes was tested at a current density of 0.1 mA cm−2 under 25 °C. As shown in Fig. 3(c), the Li/Li6PS5Cl/Li cell can perform lithium plating/stripping for 1700 h, and the cell voltage increased from 7 to 25 mV during cycling, which represents the decomposition of Li6PS5Cl.30 The cell voltage of the Li–Li symmetric battery with Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes slowly increased from the initial 7 to 8.4 mV after cycled for 13 000 h at 0.1 mA cm−2. Furthermore, the Li–Li symmetric cell with Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes was tested at a high current density of 1 mA cm−2 (Fig. S4), showing stable lithium plating/stripping for 5000 min without short circuit.

      The Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 interface after lithium plating/stripping was investigated by XRD, as shown in Fig. 5. After cycling, the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte still maintains its argyrodite major phase, while Li2S, Li3P, Li, and Li3OCl were detected. It is worth noting that Li3OCl is a lithium ion conductor and compatible toward metallic lithium, which could reduce interfacial resistance and suppress the side reaction between electrolyte and lithium.44,45 The formation of Li3P was further observed by an x-ray photoelectron spectrometer, as shown in Fig. S5, and peaks at 132.5 and 126 eV are corresponding to PS43- group and Li3P, respectively. Meanwhile, the chemical stability against wet air was improved after oxygen doping. After exposing in air at 30 °C with 35% humidity for 15 min, the amounts of generated H2S for Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 are 92.9 and 49.2 ppm, respectively [Fig. S6(a)]. The ionic conductivities of Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 decrease to 1.8 × 10−6 and 1.3 × 10−4 S cm−1, respectively [Fig. S6(b)]. The XRD peak positions and intensities are significantly changed for Li6PS5Cl after exposing in air, while Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 can maintain its structure integrity [Fig. S6(c)].

      To reveal the electrochemical performance of the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte, LiCoO2/electrolytes/Li batteries with Li6PS5Cl and Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolytes were assembled and evaluated. Figures 6(a) and 6(b) show the charge/discharge curves of LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li and LiCoO2/Li6PS5Cl/Li batteries at 0.1C, respectively. The initial discharge capacities of LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li and LiCoO2/Li6PS5Cl/Li are 135.2 and 117.1 mAh g−1, respectively. Severe polarization is observed in the LiCoO2/Li6PS5Cl/Li battery, while the LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li battery exhibited superior stability and a high reversible capacity of 117.9 mAh g−1 after 100 cycles [Fig. 6(c)]. Furthermore, the rate performance of the batteries ranging from 0.1 to 1 C is presented in Fig. 6(d). The reversible capacities for LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li batteries at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, and 1C are 129.4, 119.6, 112.8, and 103.6 mAh g−1, respectively. After the current density returned to 0.1C, a capacity of 125.2 mAh g−1 can be recovered. In contrast, the initial discharge capacity of LiCoO2/Li6PS5Cl/Li batteries at 0.1 C is 115.4 mAh g−1, and the discharge capacity at 1C is only 75.8 mAh g−1. In addition, the LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li battery was also tested at 1C after activation for the initial 5 cycles at 0.2C, delivering initial specific discharge capacities of 126.1 mAh g−1 at 0.2C and 104.6 mAh g−1 at 1C [Fig. 6(e)]. After 300 cycles, a reversible capacity of 90.7 mAh g−1 could be maintained, and a capacity retention of 86.7% is obtained, showing excellent cyclic performance at high current density.

      The EIS measurement of the batteries was conducted before and after cycling (Fig. 7). Nyquist plots of batteries after cycling consist of a semicircle as well as a low frequency sloping line, and the semicircle corresponds to the charge transfer resistance (Rct), which is associated with the interfacial resistance. The Rct of the battery with Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 after 100 cycles is much smaller than that with Li6PS5Cl, demonstrating reduced decomposition reactions occurred at the Li/Li6PS5Cl interface after oxygen doping.

      In summary, the Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05 electrolyte with a high ionic conductivity of 7.49 mS cm−1 and favorable electrochemical stability is obtained by oxygen doping and improving Cl−/S2− ratio. The Li/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li cell can maintain stable cycling for 13 000 h at 0.1 mA cm−2, and the critical current density of electrolyte significantly improved from 0.6 to 1.3 mA cm−2 after oxygen doping. Furthermore, the LiCoO2/Li6.05PS4.9O0.1Cl1.05/Li battery possesses superior cycling stability and excellent rate capability, showing 104.6 mAh g−1 with a capacity retention of 86.7% after 300 cycles at 1C. This work provides a viable solution to improve conductivity and stability against lithium for argyrodite electrolytes, which could be widely applied in high energy density all-solid-state lithium batteries.
